# BRFN Case Study Analysis



Based on the BRFN Case Study – v2, pages 1–2.

## 1. Principal Stakeholders

| Stakeholder | Needs and interests |
| --- | --- |
| Local food producers | Simple product listing, seasonal inventory updates, clear incoming orders with at least 48 hours to prepare, and weekly payments with transparent financial records. |
| Young professionals and families | Convenient access to local food without visiting multiple farms during restricted opening hours. |
| Community groups, including educational institutions, care facilities and voluntary organisations | Reliable and consistent local suppliers for catering. |
| Independent restaurants and cafés | Local ingredient sourcing with less administration when purchasing from several small suppliers. |
| BRFN marketplace operator | Coordinate marketplace activity, calculate the 5% commission, support financial transparency and traceability, and promote sustainability and community engagement. |
| External payment service | Support payment processing and exchange transaction results with the marketplace. Development and testing must use sandbox or simulated payments. |

The marketplace operator is an inferred organisational role. The case
does not specify BRFN's internal departments or staff positions.

## 2. Major Business Processes

| ID | Business process | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| BP-01 | Product listing and information management | Producers maintain product names, descriptions, prices, availability timeframes and relevant origin and food information. |
| BP-02 | Inventory and seasonal availability management | Producers update stock quantities and seasonal availability so customers can view current offers. |
| BP-03 | Product browsing and search | Customers browse categories and search for products, supported by information such as harvest dates, farm origin and seasonal availability. |
| BP-04 | Order processing and supplier coordination | The marketplace handles single-vendor and multi-vendor orders while preserving individual supplier responsibilities and delivery commitments. |
| BP-05 | Product preparation and fulfilment coordination | Producers receive order information with at least 48 hours to prepare. Customers receive clear delivery or collection arrangements. |
| BP-06 | Payment and commission processing | The system processes payments, identifies amounts attributable to producers and automatically calculates BRFN's 5% commission. |
| BP-07 | Producer settlements and financial reporting | Producers receive weekly settlements supported by transparent audit trails suitable for accounting and tax reporting. |
| BP-08 | Product traceability and quality communication | Records link customers, products and producers so affected parties can be identified and contacted quickly when quality or food-safety concerns arise. |
| BP-09 | Environmental reporting | The platform calculates food miles using postcode-based distance measurements. |
| BP-10 | Surplus produce communication and discounting | Producers communicate surplus availability and use last-minute discounts to reduce food waste. |
| BP-11 | Community education and engagement | Producers share seasonal recipes, storage guidance and farm stories. |

## 3. Organisational Responsibilities

### Producers

- Maintain product descriptions, prices, quantities and seasonal availability.
- Provide relevant farm origin, harvest, certification, allergen and
  best-before information.
- Review incoming orders and prepare their products.
- Provide information needed to communicate their fulfilment commitments.
- Contribute surplus offers and educational content where applicable.

Producer activities must be supported by intuitive mechanisms because
technical capabilities vary across the network.

### Customers

- Browse and search for products.
- Select products from one or more producers.
- Provide the information necessary to complete purchases and arrange
  delivery or collection.
- Complete payment and follow the communicated fulfilment arrangements.

Individual customers, community organisations and hospitality businesses
share these activities but have different convenience, reliability and
administrative needs.

### BRFN Marketplace Coordination

The following responsibilities are inferred from the marketplace's
required functions:

- Coordinate marketplace information and supplier-specific orders.
- Maintain transparency about supplier responsibilities and fulfilment.
- Oversee commission calculation, weekly settlements and financial records.
- Support traceability and rapid quality-related communication.
- Support environmental reporting, surplus communications and community
  education.

The case does not assign these responsibilities to named staff or
departments.

### External Payment Service

- Support sandbox or simulated payment processing.
- Return transaction results to the marketplace.

Whether the payment service also distributes producer settlements depends
on the selected integration.

### Delivery and Collection

Delivery and collection responsibilities must be clear for each supplier's
order. The case does not identify a dedicated courier, a central collection
team or BRFN as the transport provider.

### Platform Support

The platform supports the organisational responsibilities through:

- Product and inventory records.
- Browsing and search.
- Order aggregation and supplier-specific information.
- Payment and commission calculations.
- Financial audit trails.
- Customer–product–producer traceability.
- Environmental and community features.

Platform automation does not replace organisational accountability.
For example, displaying certification status does not establish who
verifies a certification claim.

## 4. Responsibility Boundaries Requiring Clarification

The case leaves some ownership and operating rules unspecified:

- Who approves listings and checks certification claims.
- Who resolves shortages, cancellations and changed orders.
- Who provides delivery or manages collection.
- How the 48-hour preparation requirement applies to last-minute surplus.
- Who initiates producer settlements and resolves payment discrepancies.
- Who coordinates quality concerns and unsuccessful customer contact.

These points require clarification rather than being treated as confirmed
responsibilities.
